    Multi-LLM PbRL

    Training Framework

    MARLHF and MADPO share a training framework. Actors generate joint responses, and the task reward ranks them to form preference pairs. The iterative trainers repeat this process against a comparator, while a replay buffer selects the pairs for each update. MARLHF and MADPO describe the two learning objectives.

    Collaboration Modes#

    collaboration_mode sets the trainable actor layout, and comparator_generation_mode sets the comparator layout in iterative training.

    Actor layoutComparator layoutJoint responses
    DecentralizedDecentralizedEach role generates its own response on both sides of the comparison.
    DecentralizedCentralizedSeparate actors are compared with one comparator that generates all roles’ responses.
    CentralizedCentralizedOne actor and one comparator each generate a complete joint response.

    The diagram compares the three supported layouts, with the actor on the left and the comparator \( \varpi \) on the right.

    In decentralized mode, each agent has its own policy and response; MADPO combines their preference margins, and MARLHF learns one reward from the joint responses. In centralized mode, one actor receives every role’s prompt and generates the complete joint response.

    All four preference trainers currently require num_turns=1 and joint_mode="aligned". Centralized actor training automatically uses a centralized comparator in the iterative trainers; a centralized actor with a decentralized comparator is not supported.

    Centralized Preference Collaboration#

    MADPOConfig, MARLHFConfig, and their iterative variants accept collaboration_mode="centralized" (default "decentralized"). One actor and one optimizer then train on the complete joint response, including the role delimiters, so later roles are conditioned on earlier ones.

    The default TaggedCentralizedComparatorAdapter writes indexed <agent_0>...</agent_0> sections. A custom centralized_comparator_adapter can redefine the joint prompt (build_prompt) and the role parser (parse_completion); the same adapter serves the actor and the comparator. Parsing happens only for the task reward and evaluation; replay, learned-reward scoring, and training retain the joint text.

    • num_agents must equal the number of task roles. Centralized training requires one actor, specified through agent_model once or one entry in agents with its tokenizer.
    • agent_devices and, with reference KL, reference_devices describe one actor; the reward model and an external comparator retain their own device settings.
    • max_new_tokens is the total joint-response budget. For two roles, this is roughly twice a per-role limit. For MARLHF, reward_max_length must also cover the joint prompt and every role’s output.
    • MADPO remains reference-free; MARLHF’s online RL stage applies the configured KL penalty.
    • The iterative trainers require comparator_centralized_agent_index=0; all comparator sources retain their defined behavior.
    • One joint completion counts as one environment step, and a preference pair uses environment_steps_per_pair (default 2). Actor checkpoints contain one actor (agent_0).

    W&B records collaboration_mode, num_roles, num_actor_models, and the effective comparator generation mode.

    Iterative Training#

    MADPOIterTrainer and MARLHFIterTrainer alternate preference collection with training. Each round compares new samples from the current policy with comparator samples at the same candidate index; either side can win, and ties are skipped. The round’s pairs are stored as \( \mathcal{D}^{(\nu)} \), and the replay buffer selects the training pairs \( \mathcal{B}^{(\nu)} \).

    The diagram unrolls one iteration \( \nu \), from sampling against the comparator to the updated policies.

    • MADPOIter runs MADPO updates on the selected pairs for num_train_epochs per iteration.
    • MARLHFIter fits a newly initialized reward model on the selected pairs, then runs the selected online RL algorithm.

    An iteration is a refresh-and-training round, not a dialog turn. Users can import the iterative configurations and trainers from comlrl.trainers.preference.

    MADPOIterConfig Parameters
    • Inherits MADPOConfig; num_train_epochs applies to each iteration
    • num_iterations: Number of refresh-and-training rounds
    • pair_selection: Adds "comparator_reward" (the iterative default), which prefers higher comparator rewards, then larger reward gaps; "all" retains every non-tied comparison and requires preference_pairs_per_sample=None
    • Comparator and replay settings: described in Comparator and Replay Buffer
    MARLHFIterConfig Parameters
    • Combines the MARLHF settings with the iterative comparator and replay settings
    • preference_scoring_reward: "task" (default) labels new comparisons with the task reward; "reward_model" uses the previous iteration’s reward model once one exists
    • reward_num_train_epochs: Reward-model training epochs per iteration

    Comparator#

    The comparator supplies the alternative responses for each comparison. comparator_policy chooses its source, and comparator_generation_mode chooses whether it generates separate role responses or one joint response.

    In each iteration, the current actors and the comparator respond to the same task, and every same-index comparison with a clear task-reward winner becomes a preference pair.

    PolicyResponse source
    current (default)Active actor weights, sampled with a separate random-number stream.
    current_copyCurrent actor weights, copied to comparator_devices for each collection phase when those differ from the actor devices.
    modelExplicit models from comparator_model_name or comparator_agents.
    historyA saved actor checkpoint from comparator_history_k iterations back; the initial policy until one exists.
    apiA remote model endpoint.

    The history source saves policy checkpoints automatically. The two sides may sample different numbers of candidates, but only indices present on both sides are compared. pair_selection="comparator_reward" ranks comparisons by comparator reward and then reward gap; it does not assume the comparator wins.

    Comparator parameters:

    • comparator_policy: "current", "current_copy", "model", "history", or "api"
    • comparator_num_candidates: Comparator sample count; defaults to preference_num_candidates
    • comparator_model_name or comparator_agents: Model sources for "model"
    • comparator_history_k: Iterations back for "history"; defaults to 1
    • comparator_devices: Device placement for local comparator models
    • policy_checkpoint_dir: Optional actor-checkpoint location; setting it also saves checkpoints for the other sources

    Comparator Generation#

    With decentralized actors, a decentralized comparator (the default) generates one response per role, while a centralized comparator receives the adapter’s joint prompt and generates all roles’ responses together. Its joint text is then parsed into role responses before pairs are built. With a centralized actor, the comparator’s joint text is retained throughout preference learning.

    Comparator generation parameters:

    • comparator_generation_mode: "decentralized" or "centralized"
    • comparator_centralized_agent_index: Role whose actor or checkpoint generates a centralized comparator’s joint response (current, current-copy, or history sources); defaults to 0, and centralized actor training requires 0
    • centralized_comparator_adapter: Trainer argument with the shared joint prompt builder and role parser

    Local Models#

    Users can specify a shared model source through comparator_model_name, or a list of sources through comparator_agents. A decentralized comparator uses one model per role; a centralized comparator uses one model, chosen by comparator_centralized_agent_index when comparator_agents lists one source per role. Sources can be model repositories or local checkpoints, as described in Model Loading.

    comparator_policy=model
comparator_model_name=/path/to/comparator-checkpoint
comparator_generation_mode=centralized
comparator_centralized_agent_index=0
comparator_devices=cuda:1

    API Comparators#

    With comparator_policy=api, the comparator queries a remote model. comparator_api_format selects the request format:

    • generic (default): The request includes the prompt, actor index, task item, candidate count, and generation settings; completions are read from comparator_api_response_field.
    • openai: Chat Completions, also used by compatible endpoints such as DeepSeek’s.
    • anthropic: Anthropic Messages API (https://api.anthropic.com/v1/messages, ANTHROPIC_API_KEY).
    • openai_responses (alias responses or codex): OpenAI Responses API (https://api.openai.com/v1/responses, OPENAI_API_KEY).

    The Anthropic and Responses formats have no n parameter, so they send one request per candidate. Requests use the standard centralized or decentralized comparator prompts.

    comparator_policy=api
comparator_api_format=anthropic
comparator_api_url=https://api.anthropic.com/v1/messages
comparator_api_model=claude-sonnet-4-20250514
comparator_api_key_env=ANTHROPIC_API_KEY

    Comparator API parameters:

    • comparator_api_url: Endpoint URL; required for "api"
    • comparator_api_format: "generic" (default), "openai", "anthropic", or "openai_responses"
    • comparator_api_model: Model name passed to the endpoint
    • comparator_api_key_env or comparator_api_key: Environment variable holding the key, or the key itself
    • comparator_api_headers: Additional request headers
    • comparator_api_key_header, comparator_api_key_prefix: Authentication header and prefix; default "Authorization" and "Bearer", with native Anthropic header handling
    • comparator_api_timeout: Request timeout in seconds; default 120
    • comparator_api_max_n_per_request: Maximum candidates per Chat Completions call, for endpoints that limit n
    • comparator_api_response_field: Completions field in a generic response; default "completions"
    • comparator_api_extra_body: Additional request-body fields

    Replay Buffer#

    The replay buffer selects which stored pairs are used for training in each round. The current round’s dataset is saved first, so it is eligible alongside earlier rounds.

    ModeEligible preference data
    current (default)All pairs from the current round, without historical sampling.
    nearest_kThe latest preference_replay_k non-empty round datasets, including the current one.
    all_historyEvery non-empty round dataset of the current trainer run.
    lambda_decayThe same history, weighted toward recent rounds.

    Each row of the diagram shows which round datasets a mode can draw from and how strongly each is weighted, with the newest round on the right.

    nearest_k means the most recent rounds, not nearest-neighbor retrieval. In nearest_k and all_history, eligible datasets have equal weight regardless of size, and historical modes sample from them rather than training on every stored pair.

    Lambda Decay#

    With age \( a=0 \) for the newest of \( K \) eligible datasets, preference_replay_lambda (\( \lambda \)) sets the selection weights:

    \[ w_a = \frac{\lambda^a}{\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\lambda^j}, \qquad 0 \leq \lambda \leq 1. \]

    \( \lambda=0 \) keeps only the newest dataset, smaller values favor recent rounds, and \( \lambda=1 \) weights every dataset equally.

    Sampling#

    Historical modes assign each of the preference_replay_sample_size draws to a dataset by weight, then sample pairs within each dataset. Sampling proceeds without repetition when the assigned count does not exceed the dataset size; larger counts cycle through shuffled copies. The combined sample is shuffled. The default sample size is the current round’s pair count, or the latest non-empty dataset’s size if the current round is empty.

    Replay Buffer parameters:

    • preference_replay_mode: "current" (default), "nearest_k", "all_history", or "lambda_decay"
    • preference_replay_k: Number of recent non-empty datasets for "nearest_k"; required in that mode
    • preference_replay_lambda: Decay parameter in [0, 1]; required for "lambda_decay"
    • preference_replay_sample_size: Pairs sampled by the historical modes, or None for the default above
    • preference_replay_dir: Optional location of the saved round datasets

    current rejects preference_replay_sample_size and preference_replay_lambda, and accepts preference_replay_k only as None or 1. The mode-specific options are preference_replay_k for nearest_k and preference_replay_lambda for lambda_decay.

    from comlrl.trainers.preference import MADPOIterConfig

args = MADPOIterConfig(
    num_agents=2,
    preference_replay_mode="lambda_decay",
    preference_replay_lambda=0.8,
    preference_replay_sample_size=256,
)

    MARLHFIterConfig accepts the same options. To replay only the latest rounds, users can set preference_replay_mode="nearest_k" and specify the number of rounds through preference_replay_k.

    Saved Preference Data#

    Each round is written to iteration_0001.json, iteration_0002.json, and so on under preference_replay_dir, with the prompts, preferred and rejected completions, rewards, and metadata. Historical samples are re-tokenized from this text. Setting preference_replay_dir to an earlier run alone does not import its files. Training checkpoints restore the replay index and its data. Actor checkpoints for the history comparator are stored separately under policy_checkpoint_dir.
